Design a Generative AI Policy That Fits Your Course

Course AI policies should reflect what students are expected to learn, practice, and demonstrate. This tool helps instructors identify an appropriate course-policy archetype, set different levels of AI use for individual assessments, specify the functions AI may perform, and add responsible-use requirements appropriate to their teaching context.

One technology, different learning purposes

The appropriate role of AI can vary substantially across courses. The examples below show how different learning priorities can reasonably lead to different policy approaches.

Illustrative Examples: Four Courses, Four Different AI Policy Logics

Scope and Basis of the Selector

Generative AI is used broadly here to include AI systems or AI-enabled functions that generate, transform, summarize, analyze, or otherwise produce substantive content in response to user input. Because these capabilities are increasingly embedded in common software, the tool focuses on permitted uses and functions rather than product lists.

The framework synthesizes analysis of 217 course AI policies, academic and external guidance, and practical experience supporting faculty members in developing and refining course AI policies. Elements of the framework and several policy approaches have also been iteratively discussed, applied, and refined with teaching colleagues across foundational, theoretical, applied, quantitative, and qualitative business courses.

The six archetypes are not a maturity ladder. They represent different pedagogical relationships between AI, learning, and assessment. A restrictive approach can be appropriate in an advanced course, and an AI-integrated approach can be appropriate in an introductory course when AI capability is itself a learning objective.

Assessment-Level Overrides

A course-level archetype establishes the default. For each major assessment, you can set an AI permission level, specify permitted AI functions, and identify any boundaries that should remain student-led.

Policy precedence: assessment-specific instructions take precedence over the course-level default for that assessment. When an assessment is silent, the course-level default applies. If the status of a tool, feature, or use is unclear, students should seek clarification before using it for assessed work.
Complete Step 1 and choose an archetype first.

Add each major assessment for which you want to specify an AI rule. Choose the overall AI level, then review the suggested AI functions that are pre-selected for that level. You can check or uncheck functions, indicate instructor-directed AI engagement, and add any student-led boundaries or other conditions.

Independent Work / No Student-Initiated AIAI does not perform the intellectual capability being assessed. Narrow support exceptions can be specified separately.
Learning & Planning SupportAI may support understanding, practice, preparation, ideation, planning, or feedback without performing the substantive assessed work.
Bounded AI CollaborationAI may perform specified functions while the intellectual work central to the assessment remains student-led.
Extensive AI CollaborationAI may contribute substantially across multiple stages, including production, with accountable human evaluation.
AI ExplorationStudents have broad discretion over tools, functions, and workflows, with transparency, verification, explainability, and human accountability.
Suggested function defaults: the tool pre-selects functions that commonly fit each assessment level. These are starting points, not rules. Editorial & Language Support is pre-selected at all levels because it is commonly permitted when it preserves the student's substantive meaning and reasoning. Uncheck it where writing or language performance is itself being assessed.

Disclosure and Citation Disclosure and citation serve different purposes. Disclosure explains how AI participated in producing the work, while citation identifies a source or object relied upon in the work. Depending on the assessment, instructors may require one or both.

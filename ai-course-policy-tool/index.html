Build a Course AI Policy from Pedagogy First

Answer six short questions to identify the course-policy archetypes that best fit your teaching context. Then set assessment-level overrides and add the responsibility clauses your course requires.

Aligned with Telfer AI Living Guidance 01: Course-Level Generative AI Policies, Version 1 (August 2026).

Core design principleStart with what students need to learn and demonstrate. The appropriate role of generative AI follows from those learning and assessment requirements. Information, material, and systems risk primarily add guardrails rather than determining the course archetype by themselves.
Scope and Basis of the Selector

Generative AI is used broadly here to include AI systems or AI-enabled functions that generate, transform, summarize, analyze, or otherwise produce substantive content in response to user input. Because these capabilities are increasingly embedded in common software, the tool focuses on permitted uses and functions rather than product lists.

The framework synthesizes analysis of 217 course AI policies, academic and external guidance, and practical experience supporting faculty members in developing and refining course AI policies. Elements of the framework and several policy approaches have also been iteratively discussed, applied, and refined with teaching colleagues across foundational, theoretical, applied, quantitative, and qualitative business courses.

The six archetypes are not a maturity ladder. They represent different pedagogical relationships between AI, learning, and assessment. A restrictive approach can be appropriate in an advanced course, and an AI-integrated approach can be appropriate in an introductory course when AI capability is itself a learning objective.

Assessment-Level Overrides

A course-level archetype establishes the default. Individual assessments can apply a different AI level when their learning purpose requires it.

Policy precedence: assessment-specific instructions take precedence over the course-level default for that assessment. When an assessment is silent, the course-level default applies. If the status of a tool, feature, or use is unclear, students should seek clarification before using it for assessed work.
